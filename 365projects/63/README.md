# Day 63: Momentum versus Mean Reversion

Choose a strategy and assets and see out-of-sample results against buy-and-hold, with overfitting warnings.

Live at https://varad-patel.github.io/365projects/63/ (part of [365 projects in 365 days](https://varad-patel.github.io/365projects/), section "Economics × Finance").

| | |
|---|---|
| Question | Does a rule that follows recent winners, or bets on a turn, still work in years it was not tuned on, and how much of the best rule's record is luck? |
| One finding | With all ten assets and a held-out test from January 2013, the best of 480 rules in 2000 to 2012 (cross-sectional momentum, 3-month look-back, holding six) had a Sharpe ratio of 0.78 but scored -0.51 afterwards, number 426 of 480, with a probability of backtest overfitting of 81%, and reshuffled training months produced a best of 0.79 on average. |
| Main limit | The assets are Treasuries and currency deposits for a dollar investor over one 27-year history, so a gap between two Sharpe ratios smaller than about 0.27 held out is within chance. |
| PPE concept | Market efficiency |
| Skills | Backtesting, validation |
| Data | Federal Reserve Board H.15 and H.10, Bank for International Settlements |
