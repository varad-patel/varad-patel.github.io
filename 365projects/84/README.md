# Day 84: Taylor Rule versus the Central Bank

Set the rule's weights and compare the rate it prescribes with what the Fed and the RBI actually set, month by month.

Live at https://varad-patel.github.io/365projects/84/ (part of [365 projects in 365 days](https://varad-patel.github.io/365projects/), section "Economics × Finance").

| | |
|---|---|
| Question | How far have the Federal Reserve and the Reserve Bank of India strayed from the rate a Taylor rule prescribes, and how much does the answer depend on the measures of inflation and slack? |
| One finding | With Taylor's weights, core PCE inflation and a Hodrick-Prescott output gap, the rule tracks the Fed over 1987 to 1992 (0.07 points apart on average), but from 2001 to June 2026 the Fed's target sat 2.3 points below it on average and more than a point below in 239 of 306 months; the RBI's repo rate sat 1.3 points below a 4%-target rule on CPI (Combined) inflation from 2014 and 1.6 points below on the CPI for industrial workers. |
| Main limit | The page uses revised data, an unobservable neutral rate and a statistical trend for potential output, so a gap between rule and bank measures the rule's assumptions as much as the bank's choices. |
| PPE concept | Rules versus discretion |
| Skills | Policy rules, time series |
| Data | Bank for International Settlements, US Bureau of Economic Analysis, US Bureau of Labor Statistics, OECD, World Bank Global Economic Monitor |
